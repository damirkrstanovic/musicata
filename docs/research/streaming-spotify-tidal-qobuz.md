# Research: Spotify / Tidal / Qobuz — Rust full-playback feasibility & legal constraints

Feasibility and legal/API constraints of integrating Spotify, Tidal, and Qobuz as music-source providers with full audio playback, self-contained in Rust, for an AGPL-3.0 local-first server. Synthesized into [prior-art §9](../prior-art.md) and [roadmap M9](../roadmap.md).

## Question

Feasibility and legal/API constraints of integrating Spotify, Tidal, and Qobuz as music-source providers with FULL AUDIO PLAYBACK into a self-hosted, AGPL-3.0, local-first music server written in Rust (called Musicata), implemented ENTIRELY IN RUST and self-contained (no external sidecar services/processes — everything in-process). Unofficial/reverse-engineered approaches are in scope but their ToS/legal risk must be documented explicitly (the goal is an honest feasibility + constraints report, not an endorsement of circumvention).

For EACH of the three services (Spotify, Tidal, Qobuz), cover:
1. Official API: does it exist, what it permits for third-party apps (catalog metadata, search, user playlists/favorites), authentication model (OAuth scopes, app registration, approval/allowlist requirements, rate limits), and crucially whether third-party apps are permitted to obtain and play actual audio streams (most official APIs forbid serving raw audio to non-certified players — confirm per service).
2. ToS / legal constraints: what the developer terms forbid (serving audio outside their SDK/player, reverse engineering, DRM circumvention), DMCA §1201 anti-circumvention exposure, and how this interacts with an AGPL open-source project that publishes its source. Note any cease-and-desist / takedown history against open-source projects.
3. Audio access reality: DRM and encryption in play (Spotify's encrypted Ogg Vorbis via the reverse-engineered protocol; Tidal/Qobuz — do they use Widevine DRM or plain encrypted URLs?), required subscription tier (Spotify Premium for librespot; Tidal HiFi/Qobuz Studio), and what audio is actually obtainable and at what quality.
4. Rust ecosystem maturity: librespot for Spotify (what it is, how it authenticates, Premium requirement, audio format, maintenance status, legal posture); whether any Rust crates exist for Tidal and Qobuz, or whether one must port from Python/JS projects (streamrip, qobuz-dl, Tidal-API, python-tidal/tidalapi, mopidy-tidal, mopidy-qobuz). Assess effort to build/port a self-contained Rust client per service.
5. How comparable open-source music servers/players integrate these: Music Assistant (librespot for Spotify and unofficial APIs for Tidal/Qobuz), Mopidy plugins (mopidy-spotify, mopidy-tidal), streamrip, Plexamp/Symfonium — their auth flows, what library they rely on, and their stated legal posture.
6. Per-service feasibility verdict for "self-contained Rust full-playback": realistic, partly realistic (e.g. metadata via official API but audio needs reverse-engineered protocol), or impractical? Rank Spotify vs Tidal vs Qobuz by feasibility and by legal risk, and identify which (if any) is the most sensible first target.

Audience: an experienced Rust engineer deciding whether/how to build this into a provider-abstraction (the server already has a MusicProvider/ProviderHandle enum-dispatch model with capabilities, and a separate player/transport layer). Be concrete about crates, protocols, and the legal bottom line.

## Bottom line

Feasibility ranks Qobuz over Tidal over Spotify for self-contained Rust full playback.

## Verified findings

### 1. Qobuz is most feasible: MD5 request-signing not DRM, signed FLAC URLs obtainable in Rust without a CDM. Reverse-engineered Rust clients deliver full audio.
*Confidence: high · verification: 3-0*

qobuz-api-rust uses MD5 signature auth, not Widevine; MoosicBox scrapes app credentials via Spoofbuz and logs in with username/password.

Sources: <https://github.com/loxoron218/qobuz-api-rust>, <https://github.com/MoosicBox/MoosicBox>, <https://github.com/iamdb/hifi-rs>
