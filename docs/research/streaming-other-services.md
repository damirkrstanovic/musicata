# Research: Other streaming / audio services — integration feasibility for a local-first server

Survey of the broader landscape beyond Spotify/Tidal/Qobuz (Deezer, YouTube Music, Apple Music, Amazon Music, SoundCloud, Bandcamp, and the open/free/self-hostable sources) — which ones actually work, with a feasibility × legal-risk ranking. Synthesized into [prior-art §9](../prior-art.md) and [roadmap M9](../roadmap.md).

## Question

Beyond Spotify, Tidal, and Qobuz (which are being researched separately), survey the BROADER LANDSCAPE of music streaming / audio services that a self-hosted, AGPL-3.0, local-first music server written in Rust (Musicata) could integrate as music-source providers — and assess which ones "seem to work" in practice (i.e., have proven, currently-functioning integrations in open-source music servers/players, a usable API or maintained reverse-engineered client, and ideally a Rust or easily-portable client). Goal: an honest feasibility ranking of additional services to consider, with the legal/ToS posture of each flagged.

Cover at least these candidates (add others you find):
1. Deezer — official API, ARL/unofficial API, DRM (Blowfish-encrypted streams), subscription tiers, open-source clients (deezer-py, streamrip, mopidy-deezer), Rust availability, what quality is obtainable.
2. YouTube Music / YouTube — ytmusicapi (metadata) + yt-dlp (audio), legality/ToS, Music Assistant's YouTube Music provider, audio quality, how robust it is, any Rust equivalent (rustypipe?).
3. Apple Music — MusicKit / Apple Music API (catalog + library), the hard requirement that playback go through Apple's MusicKit player (no raw audio to third parties), developer token model; verdict on self-contained playback feasibility.
4. Amazon Music — API availability (essentially none public), reverse-engineering status, why it's generally considered impractical.
5. SoundCloud — public/semi-public API, client_id scraping, what's streamable, open-source clients (mopidy-soundcloud), Rust feasibility.
6. Bandcamp — no official API, scraping approach, what apps do (Music Assistant?), legality of streaming purchased/free tracks.
7. FREE / OPEN / self-hostable sources that are low-legal-risk: Jamendo (CC music, real API), Free Music Archive, Internet Archive audio, SomaFM / Radio Paradise, RadioBrowser (already integrated), Funkwhale (federated, API), Subsonic/OpenSubsonic servers as a SOURCE (Navidrome/Airsonic as upstream), Plex/Jellyfin as upstream, DLNA/UPnP media servers, podcasts (RSS/Podcast Index).
8. Scrobbling / metadata-only services (not playback but relevant): Last.fm, ListenBrainz, MusicBrainz, Discogs — note these are enrichment, not sources.

For each: (a) does a working API or maintained open-source client exist right now, (b) can you actually get playable audio and at what quality / DRM, (c) subscription/auth requirements, (d) Rust ecosystem or porting effort, (e) legal/ToS risk for an open-source project, (f) how comparable projects (Music Assistant providers list, Mopidy backends/extensions, streamrip, Plexamp) integrate it.

Then RANK the additional services by a combined "feasibility × low-legal-risk × value" score and recommend which 2-3 (besides the big three) are the most sensible to support — explicitly calling out the low-risk open/free sources (Jamendo, Internet Archive, Funkwhale, upstream Subsonic/DLNA, podcasts) as likely the highest-value-per-risk wins for a local-first server.

Audience: an experienced Rust engineer with a MusicProvider/ProviderHandle capability-based provider abstraction and a separate player/transport layer. Be concrete about APIs, clients/crates, audio formats/DRM, and the legal bottom line.

## Bottom line

Beyond Spotify/Tidal/Qobuz, the broadest body of proven, currently-functioning open-source integrations clusters into two tiers. The high-value-per-risk tier is the open/free/self-hostable sources — upstream OpenSubsonic servers (Navidrome/Gonic; Funkwhale also speaks Subsonic), Jellyfin/Plex/Emby, Internet Archive, Jamendo (CC), radio (Radio Browser/SomaFM/Radio Paradise), and podcasts (Podcast Index/RSS) — all of which deliver DRM-free, full-quality playable audio (up to FLAC 24/192 on Subsonic; FLAC/MP3/OGG on Jamendo) with public/stable APIs and near-zero ToS risk, and several already have Rust-friendly or trivially-portable HTTP clients. The commercial tier (Deezer, YouTube Music, SoundCloud, Bandcamp) has working reverse-engineered clients in Music Assistant, Mopidy and streamrip, but each carries unofficial/best-effort ToS risk and DRM or scraping fragility: Deezer yields lossless 16/44.1 FLAC but only via Blowfish-decrypted ARL streams (Rust metadata client deezer-rs exists; audio decryption does not), YouTube Music is lossy AAC 256 kbps via reverse-engineered Innertube (Rust: rustypipe + the mandatory rustypipe-botguard PO-token helper since Aug 2024), SoundCloud/Bandcamp are scraping-based and lossy. Apple Music and Amazon Music are effectively infeasible for self-contained playback: Apple mandates playback through its MusicKit player (no raw audio to third parties) and Amazon Music is Widevine-DRM with an explicit ToS ban on platforms where users can install software or access the filesystem — i.e. it forbids a self-hosted server by design. Recommended additions for Musicata, in priority order: (1) upstream OpenSubsonic/Funkwhale, (2) podcasts (RSS/Podcast Index) and Internet Archive, (3) Jamendo — with Deezer as the only commercial candidate worth conditional consideration (lossless, but DRM/legal caveats).

## Verified findings

### 1. Upstream media servers (OpenSubsonic/Navidrome/Gonic, Jellyfin, Plex, Emby) are a proven 'music-source' pattern delivering DRM-free, full-quality audio (up to FLAC 24/192) — the single highest-value, lowest-risk class of additional source for a local-first server.
*Confidence: high · verification: 3-0 (merged from claims [1], [18], [19])*

Music Assistant ships dedicated providers for Subsonic, Jellyfin, Plex, and Emby (with matching codebase directories: opensubsonic, jellyfin, plex, emby), confirming the upstream-server-as-source pattern works in shipped code. The Subsonic provider streams Lossless FLAC up to 24 bit/192 kHz with no DRM (OpenSubsonic stream.view supports format=raw / download.view for byte-exact files). Caveats: MA's Subsonic provider requires the OpenSubsonic getOpenSubsonicExtensions() endpoint, so it works against Gonic/Navidrome but NOT legacy Subsonic/Airsonic/Airsonic-Advanced unless they adopt OpenSubsonic; MA's Plex provider is documented as currently unmaintained and Jellyfin maintained best-effort. For Musicata, this is the strongest fit: an OpenSubsonic client is a stable HTTP API trivially implemented in Rust, and Musicata already exposes an OpenSubsonic surface.

Sources: <https://www.music-assistant.io/music-providers/>, <https://www.music-assistant.io/music-providers/subsonic/>, <https://github.com/music-assistant/server>

### 2. Low-legal-risk open/free sources — Internet Archive, Jamendo (CC), Radio Browser, Radio Paradise, SomaFM, and podcasts (Podcast Index / RSS / gPodder / iTunes search) — are all proven, currently-shipping providers with public APIs and DRM-free playable audio.
*Confidence: high · verification: 3-0 (merged from claims [2], [21], [22])*

Music Assistant lists Internet Archive, Radio Browser, Radio Paradise, SomaFM, Podcast Index, Podcast RSS Feed, gPodder, and iTunes Podcast Search as shipped providers (MA 2.7 release, Dec 2025). Jamendo has a public REST API at api.jamendo.com needing only a free client_id (devportal.jamendo.com) for read/streaming; OAuth2 is reserved for user-specific writes. Jamendo serves multiple playable/downloadable formats: mp31 (96 kbps default for streaming), mp32 (VBR), OGG, and FLAC lossless, selectable via audioformat/audiodlformat. Mopidy corroborates with first/third-party backends for jamendo, internetarchive, somafm, and podcast. RadioBrowser is already integrated in Musicata, so this class is the natural next expansion: public APIs, DRM-free, near-zero ToS risk, mostly plain HTTP clients easily written in Rust.

Sources: <https://www.music-assistant.io/music-providers/>, <https://developer.jamendo.com/v3.0/docs>, <https://mopidy.com/ext/>

### 3. Funkwhale implements the Subsonic API, so a single OpenSubsonic/Subsonic-compatible client in Musicata can ingest both Navidrome/Gonic AND federated Funkwhale pods as sources.
*Confidence: high · verification: 3-0 (claim [20])*

Funkwhale's official developer docs expose ~26-27 Subsonic endpoints (ping, stream, getAlbum, getSong, search3, plus createPlaylist/deletePlaylist/updatePlaylist/getPlaylist(s) and star/unstar/getStarred(2)/scrobble), stating this 'enables users to listen to music stored on their Funkwhale pod through a Subsonic-compatible app.' MetaBrainz's GSoC 2025 project integrated Funkwhale streaming via this Subsonic API, confirming it works in practice. Implication: building one robust OpenSubsonic client buys Navidrome, Gonic, AND Funkwhale at once — strong leverage.

Sources: <https://docs.funkwhale.audio/developer/api/subsonic.html>

### 4. Deezer has working reverse-engineered clients (streamrip, Mopidy) yielding up to lossless 16-bit/44.1 kHz FLAC, plus a Rust metadata client (deezer-rs) — but audio requires decrypting Blowfish-encrypted streams via an ARL token, which deezer-rs does NOT do, and the legal posture is unofficial/ToS-violating.
*Confidence: high · verification: 3-0 (merged from claims [0], [5], [6], [11], [12])*

streamrip (v2.2.0, Mar 2026) downloads from Deezer at quality level 2 = 16-bit/44.1 kHz CD FLAC, plus 128/320 kbps MP3/AAC; Music Assistant ships a Deezer provider; Mopidy lists deezer-related backends. Rust ecosystem: deezer-rs and the more-maintained crates.io 'deezer' crate cover the public METADATA API (albums/artists/tracks) only — no streaming/decryption. Getting audio requires an ARL session token and Blowfish stream decryption (handled by the same author's separate 'pleezer'/music-player crates, not deezer-rs). Caveat: Deezer has tightened security and many GitHub ARL-based downloaders break periodically — robustness is fragile and the integration violates Deezer ToS. Verdict: the only commercial candidate offering true lossless, but with real DRM/legal/maintenance cost; conditional at best for an AGPL project.

Sources: <https://github.com/nathom/streamrip>, <https://www.music-assistant.io/music-providers/>, <https://github.com/tsirysndr/deezer-rs>, <https://mopidy.com/ext/>, <https://crates.io/crates/deezer>

### 5. YouTube Music has no official API; integration is reverse-engineered, best-effort, and lossy (AAC 256 kbps max, no lossless). A capable Rust client exists (rustypipe), but working stream URLs require the external rustypipe-botguard PO-token helper since Aug 2024.
*Confidence: high · verification: 3-0 (merged from claims [9], [10], [16], [17])*

MA's YTM docs state 'Youtube does not offer an official API ... everything is built on a best-effort basis' and cap quality at 'Lossy, AAC (256kbps)' (no lossless tier). ytmusicapi is the de-facto reference ('Unofficial API ... not supported nor endorsed by Google'). Rust: rustypipe is a full Innertube client for YouTube + YouTube Music (playlists, albums, artists, search, radio, track details with lyrics, charts, saved items) and exposes select_video_audio_stream() returning stream URLs. BUT since Aug 2024 YouTube requires PO tokens for web-client streams (else 403); rustypipe cannot generate them and depends on the separate rustypipe-botguard CLI binary it auto-detects on PATH. So: large catalog, free-tier usable as a source, decent Rust tooling — but lossy, ToS-grey, and operationally fragile (PO-token/botguard dependency + ongoing YouTube churn).

Sources: <https://www.music-assistant.io/music-providers/youtube-music/>, <https://crates.io/crates/rustypipe>, <https://docs.rs/rustypipe-botguard>, <https://github.com/sigma67/ytmusicapi>

### 6. SoundCloud and Bandcamp are proven open-source integrations but are scraping/reverse-engineered and lossy — SoundCloud via official-but-restricted/scraped client_id (Mopidy first-party mopidy-soundcloud, streamrip, MA), Bandcamp via no-official-API best-effort scraping (MA community provider, ~MP3 128 kbps).
*Confidence: high · verification: 3-0 / 2-1 (merged from claims [0], [3], [4], [5])*

Mopidy lists mopidy-soundcloud as a FIRST-PARTY backend (though 'on life support'), and streamrip downloads from SoundCloud; MA ships SoundCloud and Bandcamp providers. MA's Bandcamp provider (community, PR #2871) streams ~MP3 128 kbps via best-effort scraping with no official API and is flagged unofficial. Mopidy also has third-party mopidy-bandcamp. Both are lossy and ToS-grey (client_id scraping for SoundCloud; HTML scraping for Bandcamp). Note: the claim that streamrip pulls 24-bit SoundCloud was specifically REFUTED in verification — SoundCloud is lossy. Value is modest (free/promo catalogs, purchased Bandcamp tracks) and Rust support would be bespoke; lower priority than the open/free tier.

Sources: <https://mopidy.com/ext/>, <https://www.music-assistant.io/music-providers/>, <https://github.com/nathom/streamrip>, <https://github.com/music-assistant/server>

### 7. Apple Music and Amazon Music are infeasible for self-contained playback in a self-hosted open-source server: Apple mandates playback through its MusicKit player (no raw audio handoff), and Amazon Music is Widevine-DRM with an explicit ToS prohibition on open platforms where users can install software or access the filesystem.
*Confidence: high · verification: 3-0 (merged from claims [0], [13], [14], [15])*

Amazon's own docs: streams are Widevine-DRM, all manifest segments encrypted except a 30-second clear lead, 'client must support Widevine L3 or better,' and playback 'must NOT occur on any computing platform or device where the end user has the ability to install any custom software; gain access to the file system; or install alternate trusted certificates' — which by design excludes a general-purpose self-hosted server. Amazon Program Requirements add mandatory DRM, per-product Amazon certification, and 'developed and operated by you and not a third party.' MA discussion #626 (Aug 2025) reports Amazon Music integration hit a DRM roadblock with no work underway. Apple Music: MA ships an Apple Music provider (Lossy AAC 256 kbps, requires paid subscription + media-user-token, flagged 'not officially supported by Apple, use at your own risk'), but the architecture forces playback via Apple's MusicKit player — no raw audio to third parties — so a Rust transport layer cannot self-host playback. Verdict: skip both for self-contained playback.

Sources: <https://developer.amazon.com/docs/music/playback_overview.html>, <https://developer.amazon.com/docs/music/requ_AM-Program-Requirements.html>, <https://www.music-assistant.io/music-providers/>, <https://github.com/music-assistant/server/discussions/626>

### 8. Music Assistant's architecture — separate Music Providers (sources) vs Player Providers (targets), capability declaration via supported_features/ProviderFeature, and a two-phase stream path (get_stream_details() then get_audio_stream()) — directly mirrors and validates Musicata's MusicProvider/ProviderHandle + separate transport design.
*Confidence: high · verification: 3-0 (merged from claims [7], [8])*

MA defines four provider types via manifest 'type' (music/player/metadata/plugin): Music Providers are 'sources of music content,' Player Providers are 'playback targets,' plus Metadata and Plugin providers. Providers advertise capability via supported_features returning a list of ProviderFeature (callers skip unsupported ops). Playback is two-phase: get_stream_details() returns quality/format/channels, then get_audio_stream() streams raw bytes to the player. MA documents three audio patterns: external executable (e.g. librespot), direct URL (YouTube Music), and expiring HTTPS URL (Qobuz) — useful templates for Musicata's get_stream_details/get_audio_stream equivalents and its capability-advertisement convention. This confirms Musicata's existing capability-based provider/transport split is the right shape and that metadata enrichment (Last.fm/ListenBrainz/MusicBrainz/Discogs) belongs in a separate Metadata-provider lane, not as a source.

Sources: <https://developers.music-assistant.io/>

## Caveats

Time-sensitivity is the dominant caveat for the commercial tier: reverse-engineered clients depend on credentials and unpublished endpoints that break frequently — Deezer ARL/Blowfish downloaders are noted as periodically broken, and YouTube's Aug-2024 PO-token requirement already forced the rustypipe-botguard dependency; any of these can break again with no notice. Several sources are vendor/project self-documentation (Music Assistant docs, Mopidy registry, streamrip README, crate docs) — appropriate and primary for 'does an integration exist and what quality does it claim,' but not independent audits of robustness; real-world reliability of any given client should be re-verified before adoption. Maintenance status varies: MA's Plex provider is unmaintained, mopidy-soundcloud is 'on life support,' and mopidy-ytmusic (one of eleven Mopidy backends cited) was archived read-only Apr 2025 though working alternatives exist. The Subsonic-source claim is bounded: it requires OpenSubsonic (getOpenSubsonicExtensions), so legacy Subsonic/Airsonic/Airsonic-Advanced will not work unless updated. Quality figures are 'up to' ceilings and depend on upstream files and transcoding config. ToS/legal posture: every commercial provider here (Deezer, YouTube Music, SoundCloud, Bandcamp, Apple Music) is explicitly unofficial/best-effort or 'use at your own risk' per the projects' own docs — distributing such providers in an AGPL project carries DMCA/ToS exposure that the open/free sources (Subsonic/Funkwhale/Jamendo/Internet Archive/podcasts/radio) do not. AGPL-3.0 dependency-license compatibility for any new Rust crate (rustypipe, deezer, etc.) must be checked before adding, per project policy.
